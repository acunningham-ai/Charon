Self-healing

You find out from the system, not from the damage.

A harness that reads your files and runs scheduled jobs grows its own moving parts, and they break quietly. Charon watches itself. A failed run is queued, reviewed into a likely cause and ranked fixes, and put in front of you when your next session opens. It proposes; you decide. Nothing fixes itself.

The loop

Every stage stops for you before anything changes

Each step hands a decision to the next, and the only step that could change your system is the one that asks you first.

01

Failure queued

A scheduled run exits with an error. on-error.py adds it to the review queue.

02

Review

You run /harness-review --drain. A hardened, read-only pass writes a note per failure: likely cause, ranked fixes.

03

Told at session start

A session-start hook lists proposals still waiting. Silent when nothing is.

04

You pick

/harness-fix accepts, declines or defers. Nothing has changed up to here.

05

Heal check

/harness-heal checks a pre-approved, reversible fix and its preconditions. Propose-only in this release.

06

The watch

Notices when the loop itself stalls: five failures waiting, or the oldest a week old.

Why it's built this way. The review reads; it cannot act. Its engine never imports the module that applies fixes, and --selftest proves it. Its control files are sealed by hash (--seal, once, after you've read them), so a tampered prompt is detected rather than obeyed. The apply step is a closed list of reversible fixes, each needing your approval, and in this release it applies nothing at all.

The watch & the doctor

It names the limits of its own vision

/harness-doctor runs every detector on demand and prints a coverage self-report: what it checked, what it has no check for yet, and a self-test proving each detector can still fire. A dead check can't quietly read as "all healthy".

11 detectorsshipped

Health signals, observe-only

Token age, capture freshness, TODO freshness, error-log failures, a review queue nobody drains, a failure printed in a run that never reached the error log, out-of-scope writes found by the unattended-run audit, and more.

Outcome: drift is reported before it becomes damage.

/harness-doctorshipped

A self-test for every detector

Each detector is driven with a known-bad input and must fire. The report lists blind spots by name instead of hiding them.

Outcome: "all healthy" means checked, not unchecked.

/harness-fixshipped

Your decision is recorded

Accept puts the fix at the top of your work queue; decline means it stops surfacing; defer keeps it for later. Your note is kept with the decision.

Outcome: a closed item stays closed.

/harness-healpropose-only

A pre-approved fix, checked not applied

One reversible fix ships: re-run a missed capture. It is validated against a closed list, and its preconditions (auth healthy, nothing already running) are checked and shown.

Outcome: you see exactly what would run, and why it's safe, before anything does.

The honest edge

What it does not do

It does not run itself

Failures queue automatically; reviewing them is a command you run, because each review calls the model and costs tokens.

It does not apply fixes

An accepted fix is work for you, or a session you start, to implement. /harness-heal's apply step stays off until it earns trust.

The watch only reports

Every detector ships observe-only. Promoting one to ask before acting is your call, after your own shadow run.

It can be wrong

A root cause is labelled likely. The note shows its reasoning so you can disagree with it.
